# The Yard

## Venture proposition

The Yard is an AI-assisted mentorship platform for young learners. It turns expert-designed challenges and mentor guidance into structured practice, a record of learning evidence, and informed follow-up.

The first proposed commercial offer is an eight-week STEM mentorship program for ages 12–16. Families buy a defined program with a lead mentor, a project, regular group sessions, guided practice between meetings, and evidence of independent performance. The longer-term opportunity is a repeatable method and delivery system that enables a wider network of educators to provide rigorous intellectual apprenticeship.

## Customer and problem

Initial customer: families of 12–16-year-olds already purchasing STEM enrichment who want sustained guidance and greater independence between sessions.

Working problem hypothesis: inspiration and occasional expert contact do not automatically become sustained practice. Students need actionable next steps; mentors need context about attempts and difficulties; parents need evidence of progress without assuming the role of daily project manager.

The founder's own purchases and family experiences motivate this hypothesis. They are not a substitute for customer research, willingness-to-pay tests, or retention data.

## What the product does

1. **Expert-defined progression:** a sequence of domain-specific challenges, prerequisites, examples, rubrics, and escalation criteria.
2. **Student workspace:** a chosen project, scheduled practice, original attempts, revisions, and an AI assistant working within the mentor's plan.
3. **Mentor workspace:** evidence-linked review packets, the ability to inspect original work, and tools to assign or revise practice.
4. **Parent workspace:** the student's goal, learning sequence, and concrete evidence of change. Activity counts do not stand in for learning outcomes.
5. **Evaluation:** baseline, final, and follow-up tasks attempted without AI assistance and assessed against predefined criteria.

The published interface demonstrates submissions, mentor assignments, and a parent activity timeline in one browser. It uses prepared examples. It is not a deployed multiuser learning system or a live AI tutor.

## The method and democratization

The intended repeatable unit is an intellectual apprenticeship: formulate a question, study relevant knowledge, propose an explanation, design a test, interpret evidence, defend the reasoning, and revise.

Kumon is a useful operational analogy for an articulated progression, independent daily work, and trained instructors who select and guide appropriate practice. Its established method is not evidence that The Yard can reproduce the same delivery economics for open-ended inquiry. Research judgment is harder to standardize than many closed-answer exercises.

Books and other materials supply intellectual content. The proposed method sequences learning. Technology maintains continuity and assists mentors. Human relationships provide judgment, encouragement, and accountability. Inspiring environments and encounters broaden the questions students want to pursue.

The access mechanism to test has three parts:

- Experts create reusable instructional sequences and improve them from teaching experience.
- Trained educators and near-peer mentors deliver routine guidance with shared tools and specialist escalation.
- Group clinics and milestone reviews distribute scarce specialist attention across more learners.

The premium initial pilot is a validation step, not proof of global affordability. Lower-cost partner delivery, local pricing, language support, connectivity constraints, and funded cohorts would need their own tests. A prestigious campus experience also includes social and cultural benefits that software cannot simply assume it reproduces.

## Proposed pilot

- 16 learners in four groups of four; ages 12–16.
- Eight weeks focused on robotics and scientific reasoning.
- One weekly hour with a lead mentor per group.
- Three guided 20-minute practice sessions per learner per week.
- Two shared specialist clinics at milestones.
- Baseline, final independent task, and delayed follow-up.
- Proposed price hypothesis: $600 per learner for eight weeks.

Curriculum, staffing, specialist participation, enrollment, and price are unconfirmed. Recruit through trusted founder and mentor networks, conduct parent and student interviews, and seek paid commitments before expanding delivery. Prepare a clear participation agreement and operational plan before enrollment.

A small cohort can test usability, engagement, delivery effort, and willingness to pay. It cannot establish causal effects on learning or long-term character. A later adequately designed comparison would be needed to estimate the added value of AI-assisted delivery.

## Business model and illustrative economics

Start with fixed-duration programs sold directly to families. Test second-project enrollment before assuming recurring revenue. If the method and workflow prove useful, test partner delivery and institutional licensing separately.

Illustrative eight-week cohort calculation:

| Item | Assumption | Amount |
|---|---|---:|
| Revenue | 16 learners × $600 | $9,600 |
| Lead mentor sessions | 4 groups × 8 hours × $75/hour | $2,400 |
| Mentor preparation | 16 learners × 10 minutes/week × 8 weeks × $75/hour | $1,600 |
| Specialist clinics | 2 hours × $250/hour | $500 |
| Variable software and support | 16 learners × $75/program | $1,200 |
| Delivery contribution | Revenue minus the above delivery costs | $3,900 |
| Contribution/revenue | $3,900 / $9,600 | 40.6% |

These are assumptions, not realized results. Contribution excludes acquisition, payment processing, refunds, founder time outside delivery, development, fixed overhead, and taxes. It is not profit. Reducing preparation from 20 to 10 minutes is a scenario to investigate, not a claimed AI result. The public page includes an adjustable version of this model.

## Competitive context

- Polygence already offers expert research mentorship, a project workspace, mentor chat, and a sequence leading to a tangible project.
- Synthesis Teams already offers coached collaborative simulations, discussion, and learning records.
- Kumon already combines a defined progression, daily independent practice, and instructor guidance.

Consequently, projects, mentors, a dashboard, and AI assistance alone do not establish differentiation. The hypothesis to test is that a coherent method, continuity between sessions, and reliable evidence for mentor decisions improve independent learning while making human delivery more efficient.

## Advantage to earn

The founder brings Harvard/MIT education, a PhD, AI and industry experience, and the perspective of a parent purchasing enrichment. This supports an initial ability to recruit, design, and build; it is not by itself a durable moat.

Potential defensible assets would be an effective instructional method, trusted mentor relationships, a dependable delivery workflow, and permissioned examples connecting expert feedback with later independent performance. Evidence quality, lawful permission, usefulness, and demonstrated performance determine whether these assets have value.

## What must be validated

1. **Demand:** paid commitments, attendance, reasons for purchase, refund requests, repeat enrollment, and acquisition cost.
2. **Learning:** performance on unfamiliar unaided tasks, quality of explanations, appropriate help seeking, and retention of skills at follow-up.
3. **Mentor capacity:** preparation/review/support time per learner, feedback quality, and how often the system misses a need for human intervention.
4. **Delivery quality:** mentor consistency, student engagement, manageable schedules, and operational support load.
5. **Access:** affordability and delivery quality in a specific partner market; no assumption that one global price works everywhere.

Current evidence: an interface prototype, founder experience, and a proposed validation plan. No paying cohort, learning gains, time savings, retention, institutional affiliation, or proprietary dataset is claimed.

## Proposed discussion with Harvard i-lab

Seek help refining the initial customer segment, recruiting educators and learning-science collaborators, designing a credible paid pilot, and deciding whether the evidence supports a scalable software-enabled business. Do not present the landing page as traction or describe a program's acceptance as established.
